Digital Strategy

Why Your Digital Strategy Has to Come First

Nines Media Team 15 June 2026 7 min read

Every week, businesses across South Africa and beyond call us with the same story. They've been running Google Ads for three months. They're posting daily on Instagram. They just relaunched their website. And yet — nothing is working. Leads are expensive, conversion rates are low, and no one can clearly explain what success actually looks like.

The problem is almost never the tactics. It's that they skipped the strategy entirely.

Tactics Without Strategy Is Just Spending

Digital marketing tactics — paid ads, social content, SEO, email sequences — are tools. And like any tool, they only work when you know what you're building. A hammer is useless if you haven't decided whether you're building a table or a shelf.

When a business launches Google Ads without first defining its ideal customer, average deal size, and target cost per acquisition, it's essentially paying to learn what strategy would have told it upfront — at full media cost. That's an expensive education.

A strategy defines what you're trying to achieve, who you're trying to reach, and why they should choose you. Tactics are simply how you get there.

This distinction matters enormously in the South African market, where media budgets for most SMEs are tighter than in markets like the UK or US. A business in Johannesburg with a R15,000 monthly ads budget cannot afford to waste three months testing hypotheses that strategy would have resolved in week one.

Step One: Know Who You're Talking To

The most common gap we find when auditing a new client's digital presence is a vague or absent definition of their target audience. "Anyone who needs our product" is not a target audience. It's a wish.

Real audience definition goes deeper:

  • Demographics: Age, location, income bracket, life stage
  • Psychographics: Values, goals, fears, decision-making drivers
  • Behaviour: Where they spend time online, what they search for, which platforms they trust
  • Buying journey: How long does their decision take? Who else is involved?

For a Johannesburg-based B2B client, this might mean focusing tightly on financial decision-makers in Sandton and Rosebank who are actively comparing service providers. For an e-commerce brand we manage that sells into Australia and New Zealand, the audience definition shifted the entire channel mix — away from Facebook (where the SA audience was concentrated) and toward Pinterest and Google Shopping, where ANZ customers actually browse.

Audience definition is not a one-time exercise. It should be revisited quarterly, especially in markets where economic conditions shift quickly — as they do in South Africa.

Step Two: Choose Channels for the Right Reasons

Once you know who your audience is, channel selection becomes a logical decision rather than a guess. Yet most businesses choose channels based on what they're familiar with, what their competitors are doing, or what a salesperson recommended — not on where their specific customers actually are.

Here's a simple framework we use with clients:

  1. Where is your audience actively looking for solutions like yours? (Search intent → Google Ads, SEO)
  2. Where does your audience spend passive time and can be reached with awareness? (Browse intent → Meta, YouTube, TikTok)
  3. Where do your customers make peer recommendations? (Community → LinkedIn for B2B, Facebook Groups, WhatsApp)

For high-consideration B2B services, Google Search typically outperforms social advertising on cost per lead — because the prospect is actively looking for you. For impulse or lifestyle products, Meta's broad targeting can drive incredible returns. The mistake is applying the same channel mix to every business regardless of what they sell or who they sell to.

Strategy Saves Budget — with Numbers to Prove It

One of our clients — a professional services firm in Johannesburg — came to us after eighteen months of inconsistent results from a previous agency. Their monthly ad spend was R22,000 across Google and Meta. Their cost per qualified lead was R1,800.

We paused everything for two weeks to build a proper strategy. We interviewed their top ten existing clients to understand what had actually driven their purchasing decision. We mapped the buying journey. We identified that 80% of their best clients had first found them through Google Search — and that the Meta spend, which accounted for 40% of the budget, was generating almost no qualified pipeline.

We reallocated the Meta budget entirely to Google Search, rewrote the ad copy to speak to the pain points the client interviews had surfaced, and built dedicated landing pages for each service line.

Within sixty days, cost per qualified lead dropped from R1,800 to R620. Total leads increased by 34% on a smaller effective ad budget. The strategy didn't cost them money — it saved R28,000 in the first two months alone.

The Global Dimension: Strategy Scales, Tactics Don't

Nines Media works with clients not just across South Africa but in Los Angeles, London, and across Australia and New Zealand. One of the clearest patterns we see across all these markets: businesses that have invested in a documented digital strategy scale faster and more efficiently when they expand internationally.

A South African brand entering the UK market cannot simply transplant its local tactics. Audience behaviour differs. Platform preferences shift. The competitive landscape is different. But the strategic questions — who is our ideal customer, what do they value, how do they make decisions — remain the same. A business that has answered those questions rigorously for its home market has a framework it can adapt, rather than starting from zero in every new geography.

Strategy is the only thing in digital marketing that compounds across borders.

What a Practical Digital Strategy Looks Like

A usable digital strategy doesn't need to be a 60-page document. For most SMEs, a clear strategy fits on four pages and covers:

  • Business objectives (specific, measurable, time-bound)
  • Audience profiles (two to three primary personas with real research behind them)
  • Channel plan (which platforms, why, and with what budget allocation)
  • Content and creative direction (what you'll say and how it will look)
  • KPIs and measurement framework (what success looks like, how you'll track it)

With this in place, every tactical decision — which keywords to bid on, what creative to test, when to run a promotion — has a clear filter. Does this move us toward our stated objective? Does it speak to our defined audience? If not, it doesn't get done.

Start Here, Not There

The urge to jump straight into execution is understandable. Ads feel tangible. Content is visible. A new website launch feels like progress. But sustainable digital marketing results don't come from doing more — they come from doing the right things, in the right order, for the right people.

Strategy first. Always.
