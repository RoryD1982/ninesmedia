POPIA and Marketing: What South African Businesses Must Do

By Brandon Howard 3 November 2026 4 min read
Key takeaways
  • Electronic direct marketing (email, SMS, WhatsApp) generally needs consent — with a limited exception for existing customers.
  • Every marketing message must identify you and include an easy opt-out.
  • Collect only what you need, explain how you use it, and keep it secure.

The Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) affects almost every marketing activity that involves people's details: email newsletters, WhatsApp broadcasts, SMS campaigns, website forms, lead ads and customer lists uploaded to ad platforms. This guide covers the practical essentials. It's not legal advice — for specific situations, consult a legal professional or the Information Regulator's guidance.

Direct Marketing by Electronic Communication

Section 69 of POPIA governs unsolicited electronic direct marketing — email, SMS, automated calls and similar messages, which in practice includes WhatsApp marketing messages. In general:

  • You need consent from the person before sending electronic direct marketing.
  • You may ask for consent once, and only if the person hasn't previously withheld it.
  • Existing customers are an exception: you may market your own similar products or services to someone whose details you obtained in the context of a sale, provided they were given the chance to opt out when their details were collected and in every message.
  • Every message must include your identity and an easy way to opt out.

Getting Consent Properly

  • Use an unticked checkbox for marketing consent — not a pre-ticked box.
  • Explain what you'll send and how often.
  • Keep a record of when and how consent was given.
  • Keep marketing consent separate from agreeing to terms of service.
  • Honour opt-outs quickly and permanently.

Forms, Lead Ads and Tracking

  • Website forms — collect only what you need and link to your privacy notice.
  • Meta and Google lead forms — include your privacy policy link and a clear consent question if you'll market to leads.
  • Tracking pixels and analytics — explain them in your privacy notice; consider consent tools, especially with international visitors. See Meta Pixel setup.
  • Customer list uploads to ad platforms — only use data you're permitted to use for this purpose, and use platforms' hashing features.

Special Personal Information

Health, religious beliefs, race or ethnic origin, sexual life, criminal behaviour and some other categories are special personal information with stricter rules. Healthcare, insurance and financial businesses should be particularly careful not to send such data to ad platforms.

Buying Marketing Lists

Bought lists are risky under POPIA, because the people on them usually haven't consented to hear from you. Building your own consented list is safer and performs better.

A Simple Compliance Checklist

  1. Up-to-date privacy notice on your website.
  2. Consent checkboxes on forms where you'll send marketing.
  3. Opt-out links in every email and an opt-out option in WhatsApp and SMS.
  4. A record of consent and opt-outs.
  5. An appointed Information Officer (by default, the head of the business) registered with the Information Regulator where required.
  6. Agreements with suppliers who process your customer data (email platforms, agencies).

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need consent to send marketing emails in South Africa?

Generally yes. POPIA requires consent for electronic direct marketing, except to existing customers for similar products, who must be given a chance to opt out at collection and in every message.

Does POPIA apply to WhatsApp marketing?

WhatsApp marketing messages are a form of electronic direct marketing, so consent and opt-out requirements apply.

Can I buy an email list for marketing in South Africa?

It's risky. People on bought lists usually haven't consented to hear from you, which can breach POPIA. Building your own consented list is safer.
