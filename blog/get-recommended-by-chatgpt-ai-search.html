How to Get Your Business Recommended by ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and Other AI Search Tools

Nines Media Team Updated 9 October 2026 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • AI search tools retrieve web pages, then summarise them. If your content isn't crawlable, clear and corroborated elsewhere, you won't be cited.
  • Being mentioned (recommended) depends heavily on what other sites say about you — directories, reviews, lists, media and partners.
  • Being cited (linked as a source) depends on clear, specific, answer-first pages with their own URLs and structured data.
  • Allow AI search crawlers, keep business facts consistent everywhere, and track AI referrals in GA4.

A growing share of buyers no longer start with ten blue links. They ask ChatGPT "which accounting firms in Pretoria are good for small businesses?", or read a Google AI Overview before deciding who to call. Google launched AI Mode for South African users in August 2025, so this shift is happening here, not just overseas.

That raises a new question for every business: when AI answers a question in your category, does it mention you?

How AI search tools choose what to say

Most AI search experiences — ChatGPT search, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and Perplexity — work in a similar way:

  1. They interpret the question and often break it into several sub-searches.
  2. They retrieve web pages from a search index.
  3. They read and summarise the most relevant passages.
  4. They cite some sources and name businesses that appear consistently across what they retrieved.

Two outcomes matter:

  • Citation — your page is used and linked as a source.
  • Mention or recommendation — your business is named in the answer, often because other pages mention you.

They need different work.

Part 1: Make your website easy for AI to retrieve and cite

Give every answer its own URL

AI tools retrieve pages, not sections hidden behind tabs or loaded by JavaScript. Every service, location and article should have its own crawlable URL with server-rendered HTML.

Answer first

Put a direct, specific answer at the top of each page — a two-to-four sentence summary or a short list of key takeaways — then go into detail. AI tools extract passages; make yours worth extracting.

Be specific

"We offer affordable solutions" gives an AI nothing to use. "Website builds start from R15,000 and take three to six weeks" does. Prices, timeframes, locations, processes and data make content citable.

Structure clearly

Use descriptive headings phrased like real questions, short paragraphs, lists, comparison tables and FAQ sections.

Add structured data

Mark up your Organization or LocalBusiness details, articles, FAQs, products and reviews (where genuine) with schema.org. It helps machines understand who you are and what each page is about.

Allow the right crawlers

Check your robots.txt and any firewall or CDN bot settings. To appear in AI search, you generally need to allow search crawlers such as Googlebot, Bingbot (which supports Copilot and other services), OAI-SearchBot (ChatGPT search), PerplexityBot and Claude-SearchBot. Training crawlers such as GPTBot or Google-Extended are a separate choice; blocking them does not remove you from search results.

Consider llms.txt

An llms.txt file is a short plain-text summary of your business and key pages for AI tools. It is a proposed standard rather than a confirmed ranking factor, but it's simple to add.

Part 2: Get mentioned where AI looks

This is the part most businesses miss. When someone asks "best digital marketing agency in Johannesburg", AI tools often summarise directory listings, comparison articles, review sites and media coverage — not just company websites. To be recommended:

  • Be listed on reputable directories for your industry: for agencies, platforms like Clutch, The Manifest and DesignRush; for local services, Google, Bing Places, Apple Maps and South African industry associations.
  • Earn reviews on Google and relevant review platforms, with detail about what you did and where.
  • Get into comparison and "best of" articles — through PR, partnerships, awards and genuinely useful contributions.
  • Publish expertise elsewhere — guest articles, podcast appearances, conference talks, industry media such as Bizcommunity.
  • Keep facts consistent — the same name, address, phone number, services and description everywhere. Contradictions reduce confidence.
  • Be active where conversations happen — LinkedIn, community forums and industry groups, contributing genuinely.

Part 3: Measure AI visibility

  1. Track AI referrals in GA4 — sessions from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, gemini.google.com, copilot.microsoft.com and similar.
  2. Run a monthly prompt check — ask the main AI tools 10–20 questions your customers would ask, and record whether you're mentioned, cited or described accurately.
  3. Watch Search Console — AI features appear within Google Search reporting, and branded search growth is a good proxy for wider visibility.
  4. Use AI visibility tools if budget allows — several SEO platforms now track brand mentions in AI answers.

A 30-day AI visibility checklist

  • Audit robots.txt and firewall rules for AI search crawlers.
  • Make sure every key service and article has its own indexable URL.
  • Add key-takeaway summaries and FAQs to your most important pages.
  • Add Organization/LocalBusiness, Article and FAQ structured data.
  • Update and align your Google Business Profile, social profiles and directory listings.
  • Claim profiles on the main directories for your industry.
  • Ask ten recent clients for detailed reviews.
  • Publish one genuinely useful, data-backed piece on a question your buyers ask AI.
  • Set up an AI referral segment in GA4.

What doesn't work

  • Stuffing pages with keywords or hidden text for AI.
  • Mass-producing thin AI-written articles.
  • Fake reviews or self-published "top 10" lists that rank yourself first without substance — they erode trust with people and machines.

The bottom line

AI search rewards the same things good marketing always has: being clear about what you do, being genuinely useful, and having other people vouch for you. The difference is that AI condenses that reputation into one answer — and you either appear in it or you don't.

Frequently asked questions

How does ChatGPT decide which businesses to recommend?

When ChatGPT searches the web, it retrieves pages relevant to the question and summarises them. Businesses that appear on multiple trusted pages — directories, reviews, comparison lists, media, their own clear website — are more likely to be named. Without web search, it relies on what it learned in training, which also reflects how widely a business is discussed online.

What is generative engine optimisation (GEO)?

GEO is the practice of making your brand and content more likely to be found, cited and recommended by AI search tools like ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Gemini, Copilot and Perplexity. It builds on SEO but puts more weight on clear answers, structured facts and third-party mentions.

Is GEO different from SEO?

It overlaps heavily. The same foundations — crawlable pages, helpful content, authority and accurate business information — drive both. GEO adds emphasis on answer-first formatting, entity consistency, off-site mentions and monitoring how AI tools describe you.

Does llms.txt help with AI search?

llms.txt is a proposed standard: a plain-text file summarising your site for AI tools. It is cheap to add and may help some tools, but major AI search engines have not confirmed they rely on it, so treat it as a nice-to-have, not a strategy.

How do I know if AI tools are sending me traffic?

In GA4, look for referrals from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, gemini.google.com and copilot.microsoft.com. Also ask the AI tools your customers' questions regularly and record whether and how your business appears.

Is Google AI Mode available in South Africa?

Yes. Google launched AI Mode in Search for users in South Africa in August 2025, alongside AI Overviews that already appeared on many searches.
