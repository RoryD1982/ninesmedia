How to Choose a Digital Marketing Agency in Johannesburg (and South Africa)

Brandon — Nines Media 2026-08-15 8 min read

Choosing the wrong digital marketing agency is an expensive mistake. You'll typically sign a 6–12 month contract, hand over your ad accounts and social profiles, and spend months rebuilding if things go wrong. This guide gives you a practical framework for finding the right agency in Johannesburg or anywhere in South Africa — without being dazzled by slick presentations.

Start With What You Actually Need

Before approaching any agency, be clear about what you're trying to achieve. "Get more leads" is not a brief. "Generate 30 qualified enquiries per month within a R25,000 monthly budget, targeting homeowners in Gauteng aged 35–55" is a brief. The clearer you are about your goals, target market, budget, and timeline, the easier it is to evaluate whether an agency's proposal actually addresses your problem.

Certifications That Actually Mean Something

In digital marketing, most certifications are self-issued and meaningless. Two actually matter:

Google Partner / Premier Partner — requires passing Google's certification exams AND meeting ongoing performance and spend thresholds. Agencies must requalify each year. A Google Partner badge on a website means someone at the agency passed a real test and the agency manages campaigns to Google's standards.

Meta Business Partner — similar principle. Meta certifies agencies that meet spend thresholds and pass their assessments. Relevant if you're running Facebook and Instagram advertising.

Nines Media holds both. If an agency can't show you either of these for their relevant services, ask why.

Questions to Ask in the Initial Meeting

  • Can I see examples of campaigns you've run for businesses similar to mine?
  • Who will actually be managing my account day-to-day?
  • How do you measure success? What does your reporting look like?
  • Will I own my ad accounts, social profiles, and website? (The answer must be yes.)
  • What happens if results aren't what we expected after 3 months?
  • How often will we speak? Who do I contact with questions?
  • How do you handle ad spend — does it go through you, or directly to Google/Meta?

How to Compare Quotes Fairly

Don't compare on price alone. Compare on scope. If Agency A quotes R8,000/month and Agency B quotes R15,000/month, the question isn't "why is B more expensive?" — it's "what does B include that A doesn't?" Get both agencies to list their deliverables in writing: number of campaigns, ad sets, creatives produced, reporting frequency, and what's excluded.

Also compare what's included in terms of ad spend. Some agencies charge a management fee and you pay ad spend separately to Google/Meta. Others bundle ad spend into a package. The second model is more convenient but less transparent — always ask how much of your money actually reaches the ad platforms.

Red Flags That Suggest You Should Keep Looking

  • They promise guaranteed results or guaranteed rankings
  • They're vague about who specifically will work on your account
  • They can't show you their own digital presence (a digital marketing agency with a poor website is a warning sign)
  • They resist giving you ownership of your accounts and data
  • Their case studies are all "confidential" or lack any metrics
  • There's heavy pressure to sign immediately
The right agency will ask more questions than they answer in the first meeting — because they need to understand your business before they can propose a strategy.
