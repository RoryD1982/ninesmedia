How to Choose a Digital Marketing Agency in Johannesburg (and South Africa)

Nines Media Team Updated 9 October 2026 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Choose an agency on proof of results in businesses like yours, not on the size of its service list.
  • Verify certifications — Google Partner and Meta Business Partner status can be checked in each platform's directory.
  • You must own your ad accounts, website, analytics and creative. Non-negotiable.
  • Ask how they will measure success in the first 90 days, and who will actually work on your account.

Choosing a digital marketing agency is one of the bigger decisions a South African business makes. Johannesburg alone has hundreds of agencies, from one-person operations in Fourways to large groups in Sandton and Rosebank, and nearly all of them promise growth. Some deliver it. Many deliver reports.

This guide gives you a practical way to separate the two.

1. Start with your goal, not their service list

Before you speak to anyone, write down what success looks like in twelve months: more qualified leads, more online sales, entering a new region, or building brand awareness. An agency that is brilliant at brand campaigns may not be the right partner for cost-per-lead performance, and vice versa.

Write down your current numbers too — monthly enquiries, cost per lead, conversion rate, average sale value. The best agencies will ask for them.

2. Look for proof in businesses like yours

Case studies matter most when they come from businesses with similar customers, price points and sales cycles. Ask:

  • What was the starting point, and what changed?
  • Which metrics improved — leads, revenue, cost per acquisition?
  • Over what time frame, and with what budget?
  • Can I speak to a current client?

Some agencies, including ours, anonymise case studies to protect client confidentiality. That is reasonable — but they should still be able to walk you through the numbers and the approach in detail.

3. Verify certifications

  • Google Partner status means the agency meets Google's spend, performance and certification requirements. You can check it in Google's Partner directory.
  • Meta Business Partner status can be verified in Meta's partner directory.

Certifications are not a guarantee of results, but claiming status an agency does not hold is an immediate red flag.

4. Insist on owning everything

You should own:

  • Your Google Ads, Meta Business Manager and LinkedIn ad accounts, with billing in your name.
  • Your Google Analytics, Search Console and Google Business Profile.
  • Your domain, hosting and website files.
  • All creative assets produced for you.

The agency should be added as a user. If an agency insists on running your ads through its own accounts, you lose your data, history and audiences the day you leave.

5. Understand who will actually do the work

A senior strategist often leads the pitch, then a junior runs the account. Ask:

  • Who is my day-to-day contact, and what is their experience?
  • How many accounts does each manager handle?
  • How often will we meet, and who attends?

6. Check how they measure and report

Good agencies report on business outcomes: leads, sales, cost per acquisition and return on ad spend. Ask to see a sample report. If it is full of impressions, reach and likes with no line connecting spend to revenue, keep looking. Our guide to measuring digital marketing ROI explains what good reporting includes.

7. Compare pricing on scope, not just the number

Two quotes of R12,000 can contain completely different amounts of work. Ask each agency to list monthly deliverables, hours, and the split between management fee and ad spend. Our digital marketing pricing guide lists typical South African ranges.

Red flags to watch for

  • Guaranteed rankings or guaranteed numbers of leads.
  • Long lock-in contracts before results are proven.
  • Ad accounts set up in the agency's name.
  • No conversion tracking plan in the proposal.
  • Unwillingness to share access to analytics.
  • A one-size-fits-all package with no questions about your business.

Questions to ask in the first meeting

  1. What would you do in our first 90 days?
  2. What results would you expect at our budget, and by when?
  3. How do you track leads from ad click to sale?
  4. Who will manage our account, and how many others do they manage?
  5. What do you need from us to succeed?
  6. How do we exit if it isn't working?

Local agency or remote?

A Johannesburg agency brings practical advantages: understanding of local buying behaviour and platforms, familiarity with South African tools such as PayFast and WhatsApp Business, regulatory awareness under POPIA, and the option of in-person strategy sessions. That said, relevant experience and transparency matter more than location.

Agency, freelancer or in-house?

Freelancers can be excellent value for a single channel. In-house teams suit businesses where marketing is constant and complex. Agencies give you a bench of specialists without the cost of hiring each one. We compare the options in building an in-house digital team vs outsourcing.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find a good digital marketing agency in Johannesburg?

Shortlist three to five agencies from referrals, Google reviews and directories such as Clutch or The Manifest. Then compare them on relevant case studies, verified Google and Meta partner status, account ownership terms, reporting and who will run your account day to day.

What questions should I ask a digital marketing agency?

Ask what results they have achieved for similar businesses, how they track leads and sales, who will manage your account, what happens in the first 90 days, how they charge, and whether you will own all accounts and assets.

Is it better to hire a local Johannesburg agency?

Local knowledge helps with South African platforms, payment gateways, consumer behaviour and in-person strategy sessions. But proven expertise in your industry and channels matters more than postcode — many agencies serve clients nationwide.

How much does a digital marketing agency cost in South Africa?

Most SMEs pay R5,000–R25,000 per month in agency fees plus ad spend. See our full guide to digital marketing packages and pricing in South Africa for a detailed breakdown.

Should I choose a full-service agency or specialists?

Full-service agencies make sense when you want strategy, creative and media working together under one team. Specialists can be better for a single complex channel, such as enterprise SEO. Many SMEs prefer one accountable partner.
