How to Get Leads from Google Ads in South Africa: A Practical Playbook

By Brandon Howard Brandon — Nines Media 2026-07-25 9 min read

Running Google Ads and getting leads from Google Ads are two very different things. Many South African businesses are spending R10,000–R30,000 per month on Google Ads and seeing clicks — but not many qualified leads. This playbook covers the specific levers that separate campaigns that generate a steady pipeline from campaigns that burn budget.

1. Start With the Offer, Not the Ad

Before writing a single ad, ask: why would someone who has never heard of us call us instead of the next result on Google? If your answer is "because we're good" or "because we have experience", that's not an offer — that's a baseline expectation. A compelling offer might be: a free site inspection, a no-obligation quote within 2 hours, a first-session discount, or a free audit. Your offer needs to reduce the risk of the first step for a stranger.

In the South African market, WhatsApp as a response channel has become a significant differentiator. Many prospects are more comfortable sending a WhatsApp than filling in a form or making a call. If you can incorporate a WhatsApp CTA into your landing page, your contact rate will likely increase.

2. Match Your Keywords to Intent

Not all keywords are equal. "plumber" has lower intent than "emergency plumber Sandton" — and "emergency plumber Sandton cost" signals even higher commercial intent. Organise your campaigns by intent tier:

  • High intent (buy/hire now): "[service] + [city]", "[service] near me", "best [service] [city]"
  • Commercial intent (researching): "[service] cost", "[service] prices", "how much does [service] cost"
  • Informational (less valuable for lead gen): "how to [DIY task]", "what is [service]"

Focus your budget on high and commercial intent keywords first. Add informational terms only if you have budget to spare and content specifically designed for that intent.

3. Build a Landing Page, Not a Homepage

Sending Google Ads traffic to your homepage is one of the most common and costly mistakes in SA digital marketing. Your homepage is designed for multiple audiences with multiple goals. A landing page is designed for one audience with one goal: to submit a lead form or call.

A high-converting landing page for a South African service business includes: a clear headline that matches the ad (if the ad says "Emergency Plumber Sandton", the page should say "Emergency Plumber in Sandton"); social proof (Google reviews, accreditations, client logos); a specific offer; a short lead form (name, phone, brief message — not 12 fields); and a click-to-call button prominent on mobile.

4. Track Everything — Including Calls and WhatsApp

If you can't measure it, you can't improve it. At minimum, track: form submissions (via Google Tag Manager), phone calls from the website (via call tracking numbers or Google Forwarding Numbers), and WhatsApp clicks (track the click event in GA4). Without this data, you're optimising Google Ads toward clicks — not leads.

Google Ads' Smart Bidding strategies (Target CPA, Maximise Conversions) become dramatically more effective once you feed them real conversion data. An account tracking form fills and calls consistently will outperform one tracking only page views.

5. Follow Up Fast

This is outside Google Ads but critical to its ROI: the speed of your response to a lead is a major predictor of whether it converts. Research consistently shows that lead response within 5 minutes dramatically outperforms response after 30 minutes. In the South African market, a WhatsApp or call within minutes of a lead coming in is a genuine competitive advantage — because most businesses don't do it. If your follow-up is slow, you're effectively paying Google to generate leads for your competitors who call first.
