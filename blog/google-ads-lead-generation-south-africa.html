How to Get Leads from Google Ads in South Africa: A Practical Playbook

Nines Media Team Updated 9 October 2026 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Lead generation is a system: intent keywords → matching ad → focused landing page → tracked conversion → fast follow-up.
  • Track calls, forms and WhatsApp clicks — and feed qualified leads back into Google so it optimises for quality, not volume.
  • A clear offer (free quote, consultation, assessment) lifts conversion rates more than any bid change.
  • Respond within minutes. Slow follow-up wastes more ad budget than bad keywords.

Most South African businesses running Google Ads have the same complaint: plenty of clicks, not enough enquiries — or enquiries that go nowhere. Generating leads isn't about a single trick. It's a chain, and it is only as strong as its weakest link.

The Google Ads lead generation chain

  1. Keyword — someone searches with intent.
  2. Ad — your ad matches their search and promises what they want.
  3. Landing page — the page delivers on the promise and makes contact easy.
  4. Conversion — they call, message or submit a form, and you track it.
  5. Follow-up — your team responds fast and qualifies the lead.
  6. Feedback — you tell Google which leads became customers.

Here's how to strengthen each link.

1. Target searches that signal buying intent

Separate your keywords into tiers:

  • High intent: "conveyancing attorney Pretoria quote", "solar installation price Johannesburg".
  • Medium intent: "best solar installers Gauteng".
  • Research intent: "how does solar work" — usually not worth paying for on a lead budget.

Spend most of your budget on high intent. Add negative keywords for job seekers, students and DIY searches.

2. Make the ad a promise

Your ad should repeat the searcher's language and make a clear offer: "Free Solar Quote in 24 Hours", "Same-Week Consultations — Sandton". Use call assets and location assets, and mention proof such as review ratings or years of experience.

3. Give them a reason to enquire now

A strong offer beats a generic "Contact us":

  • Free quote or site assessment
  • Free 15-minute consultation
  • Price guide or calculator
  • Same-day or same-week availability

4. Build landing pages that convert

  • One page per service theme, with a headline that matches the ad.
  • Form above the fold with as few fields as you can get away with.
  • Click-to-call and WhatsApp buttons that work on mobile.
  • Reviews, client logos, accreditations and photos of real work.
  • Answers to common objections: price, timing, process, guarantees.
  • Fast load time on mobile data.

Many well-built pages convert 5–15% of visitors. If yours is below 3%, the page is the place to start, not the bids.

5. Track every type of lead

South African customers enquire in different ways, so track them all:

  • Form submissions (thank-you page or form events)
  • Calls from ads (Google forwarding numbers) and calls from the website
  • WhatsApp button clicks
  • Bookings through calendars or booking tools

Mark only genuine enquiries as primary conversions, so Google doesn't optimise for page views or scrolls.

6. Optimise for lead quality, not just volume

Cheap leads that never buy are expensive. To improve quality:

  • Add one or two qualifying questions to forms (budget, location, timeline).
  • Import offline conversions — mark leads as qualified or won in your CRM and send that data back to Google Ads.
  • Bid on value: give higher values to the services or lead types that close best.

7. Follow up in minutes, not days

The fastest responder usually wins the job. Set up instant email or WhatsApp notifications, auto-replies that confirm receipt, and a named person responsible for every lead. Track response times — they are a marketing metric.

8. Measure cost per customer

Cost per lead is useful. Cost per customer is what matters. If 60 leads cost R10,000 and 12 become customers, your cost per customer is about R833. Compare that to the customer's value to decide whether to scale. Our guide to measuring digital marketing ROI explains how.

Where most accounts go wrong

When we audit lead generation accounts, the most common problems are homepage landing pages, untracked calls and WhatsApps, broad keywords with no negatives, and no feedback loop on lead quality. Frequently asked questions

How many leads can I expect from Google Ads?

Divide your ad spend by your average CPC to estimate clicks, then multiply by your landing page conversion rate. For example, R10,000 at R10 per click gives 1,000 clicks; at a 6% conversion rate that is about 60 leads.

What is a good conversion rate for a Google Ads landing page?

For lead generation, many well-built landing pages convert between 5% and 15% of visitors. Below about 3% usually indicates a mismatch between the ad, the offer and the page.

Why am I getting junk leads from Google Ads?

Common causes are broad match keywords without negatives, Performance Max or Display placements sending low-intent traffic, and forms that are too easy to submit. Add qualifying questions, tighten keywords and import qualified-lead data so Google learns what a good lead looks like.

Should I use Google lead form assets?

Lead form assets let people submit details without leaving Google. They can lower cost per lead but often reduce quality, so test them alongside your landing page and compare how many become customers.

How fast should I follow up on leads?

As fast as possible — ideally within five minutes during business hours. Use instant notifications, WhatsApp auto-replies and a clear owner for every lead.
