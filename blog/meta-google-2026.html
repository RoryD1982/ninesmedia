Paid Advertising

Getting the Most from Meta & Google Ads in 2026

Nines Media Team 10 May 2026 8 min read

If you're managing paid advertising in 2026, you're navigating platforms that have changed more in the last eighteen months than in the previous five years. Google has pushed automation harder than ever with Performance Max. Meta has essentially handed audience targeting over to its AI through Advantage+. Video has become non-negotiable on both platforms. And for South African advertisers managing rand-denominated budgets, the cost pressure is real.

This isn't a beginner's guide to running ads. It's what we're seeing actually work — right now — across the South African businesses and international clients we manage daily at Nines Media.

Google in 2026: Performance Max Is Everywhere, But It Needs Managing

Performance Max (PMax) campaigns now dominate Google's recommended ad formats, and for good reason — when set up correctly, they consistently outperform siloed Search or Shopping campaigns on volume metrics. But "set it and let Google optimise" is not a strategy. It's a starting point with risks.

What PMax does well

PMax campaigns run across Search, Display, Gmail, Maps, YouTube, and Discover from a single campaign. Google's machine learning allocates budget to whichever placement is converting at the lowest cost, in real time. For businesses with sufficient conversion data (generally 50+ conversions per month), this tends to improve cost per result over time without manual bid adjustments.

What you still need to control

Left to its own devices, PMax will find conversions — but not necessarily the right ones. The two levers that matter most are asset quality and audience signals.

  • Asset quality: PMax generates ad combinations from the images, headlines, descriptions, and videos you upload. Low-quality creative produces low-quality combinations. Give it your best photography, your sharpest headlines, and — critically — at least one vertical video (9:16, minimum fifteen seconds) to unlock YouTube inventory properly.
  • Audience signals: Upload your customer list, remarketing audiences, and high-intent custom segments. These don't restrict who PMax can reach, but they tell Google's algorithm where to start looking for similar users, which dramatically speeds up the learning phase.
SA-specific note South African advertisers should pay close attention to placement exclusions. PMax will, by default, serve Display inventory across a broad network that includes low-quality placements. Regularly audit your placement reports and exclude irrelevant app categories or sites that are burning impressions without conversions.

Google Search: Still the Most Valuable Intent Channel

Despite PMax's dominance in Google's push, standard Search campaigns remain the most reliable channel for high-intent, high-conversion traffic — particularly for professional services, B2B, and any product where customers are actively searching before buying.

The biggest shift in Search over the past year is AI Overviews and the continued reduction of traditional ad positions on results pages. This makes Quality Score more important than ever. A high Quality Score (reflecting strong ad relevance, expected click-through rate, and landing page experience) is your best protection against rising CPCs.

A Google Ad that sends traffic to your homepage is almost always a mistake. Build dedicated landing pages for each campaign theme — pages with one clear message, one conversion goal, and a call-to-action that is immediately visible.

For South African advertisers in particular, landing page load speed is critical. With a large percentage of users on mobile data connections and data costs remaining higher than in many other markets, a landing page that takes more than three seconds to load will lose a significant portion of its visitors before they even see your offer.

Meta in 2026: Advantage+ Is Running the Show

Meta's Advantage+ suite — which covers audience targeting, placements, creative, and budget allocation — now represents the default setup for most Meta campaigns, and in most cases, it performs better than heavily manually-controlled campaigns did two years ago. The caveat is the same as with PMax: the algorithm is only as good as what you feed it.

Advantage+ Shopping Campaigns for e-commerce

For e-commerce clients, Advantage+ Shopping campaigns (ASC) have become our go-to recommendation. They combine prospecting and retargeting in a single campaign, with Meta deciding in real time which audiences and placements will convert most efficiently. We've seen ASC outperform manually segmented funnel campaigns by 20–40% on ROAS for several clients — including one Johannesburg-based fashion retailer and two e-commerce brands we manage into the Australian market.

Creative is now the targeting

This is the most important strategic shift in Meta advertising right now. With broad targeting and Advantage+ audiences, the creative itself is effectively doing the work of audience selection. A video that resonates with your ideal customer will naturally attract more of that customer through Meta's signals — even if the targeting parameters are extremely broad.

This means your creative production quality, variation, and testing cadence matters more than it ever has. Run a minimum of three to five distinct creative variations per campaign. Test different hooks in the first three seconds of video. Refresh creative every four to six weeks, or earlier when frequency metrics start climbing.

Video: Non-Negotiable on Both Platforms

Both Google and Meta's algorithms heavily favour video creative in 2026. On Meta, Reels placements now account for a majority of impression volume in most campaigns, and video ads consistently achieve lower CPMs than static image ads across most audience categories. On Google, YouTube pre-roll inventory is a core part of PMax reach, and bumper ads remain one of the most cost-efficient awareness formats available.

You don't need a production studio. Short-form video that feels authentic and native to the platform often outperforms polished brand videos. A thirty-second video shot on a phone, edited for vertical format, with clear text overlays (for viewers watching with sound off — which is most of them) will beat a sixty-second produced spot that's been repurposed from landscape.

Format guide for 2026 For Meta: 9:16 vertical video, 15–30 seconds, hook in the first 3 seconds, text overlay, closed captions. For YouTube: 6-second bumpers for awareness, 15–30 second skippables for consideration, full 60–90 seconds only for high-intent remarketing audiences.

Remarketing: Still One of the Highest-ROI Tactics Available

With browsers restricting third-party cookies and tracking becoming more fragmented, remarketing is harder to implement than it was three years ago — but it remains one of the highest-return tactics in any paid media account.

The key is first-party data. Build your remarketing audiences from your own CRM lists, website visitor segments (segmented by page visited or time on site), and previous purchaser lists. These audiences are far more reliable than pixel-based audiences that may be incomplete due to consent or tracking gaps.

For international campaigns — particularly the UK, Australia, and New Zealand markets we manage — consent mode and proper privacy compliance is not optional. Campaigns built on clean, first-party data consistently outperform those relying on broad pixel tracking.

Budget Allocation: A Starting Framework

There's no universal budget split, but for a business with a balanced acquisition and retention objective, this is a reasonable starting framework to test and adjust from:

  • Google Search (brand + non-brand): 35–40% of total paid budget
  • Meta (prospecting via Advantage+): 30–35%
  • Remarketing (Google Display + Meta retargeting): 15–20%
  • YouTube / video awareness: 10–15%

For South African businesses with smaller monthly budgets (under R20,000/month), we typically recommend concentrating spend on one or two high-intent channels rather than spreading thinly across all of them. A well-run Google Search campaign and a focused Meta Advantage+ campaign will outperform four channels at sub-optimal budgets.

The Landing Page Problem No One Talks About Enough

The single change we make most often when taking over a paid media account — regardless of whether it's a local Johannesburg business or an international client — is fixing the landing page. Ad optimisation has a ceiling. Landing page optimisation does not.

A landing page that converts at 3% and a landing page that converts at 6% on the same ad spend doubles your leads without increasing your budget. For South African businesses where rand-denominated media costs are sensitive to exchange rate fluctuations, this is the most cost-effective lever available.

At minimum, every landing page should have: a headline that directly matches the ad that sent traffic there, a single clear call-to-action above the fold, social proof (reviews, client logos, or a specific result), and a form or contact mechanism that works on mobile. That's the floor, not the ceiling.

What to Measure in 2026

Platform-reported metrics — impressions, clicks, even platform-attributed conversions — are increasingly unreliable as tracking gaps widen. We recommend building a measurement framework that triangulates across platform data, CRM data, and business outcomes:

  • Platform ROAS or CPA: directional, not definitive
  • CRM-verified leads or sales: the source of truth for B2B or service businesses
  • Revenue per R spent: the only metric that ultimately matters
  • Incrementality tests: periodically switch off channels to understand true contribution

Paid advertising in 2026 rewards those who understand both what the platforms have automated and what still requires human judgment. The most successful accounts we manage combine strong platform automation with deliberate creative, rigorous measurement, and clean first-party data. Get those three right, and the platforms will do the rest.
