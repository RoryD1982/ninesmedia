Digital Marketing for Aesthetic and Medical Spa Clinics

By Brandon Howard 3 November 2026 4 min read
Key takeaways
  • Aesthetic medicine faces stricter rules than beauty — HPCSA rules, the Medicines Act and platform policies all apply.
  • Educational content and consultation bookings work better than promotional discounts.
  • Before-and-after images and testimonials need particular care.

Aesthetic clinics sit between healthcare and beauty. Demand is strong and clients research heavily — but the rules are stricter than for salons. Marketing has to build trust while staying compliant.

The Rules You Need to Know

  • HPCSA ethical rules apply to registered practitioners: advertising must not be unprofessional, untruthful, deceptive or misleading, and canvassing — such as claiming superior quality of service — is prohibited.
  • Prescription medicines (such as botulinum toxin products) are restricted in advertising to the public under South African medicines legislation. Advertise consultations and the practitioner, not the medicine.
  • Google and Meta policies restrict ads for prescription drugs and some cosmetic procedures, and limit targeting in health categories.

Check specific campaigns with your professional body or a compliance adviser before running them. See also digital marketing for doctors.

Content That Builds Trust

  • Practitioner profiles: qualifications, registration and experience.
  • Treatment explainers: what's involved, suitability, recovery, risks.
  • Honest FAQs: cost ranges, how many sessions, what results realistically look like.
  • Short videos explaining consultations and the clinic environment.

Before-and-After Images and Testimonials

These are powerful but risky. Use only with written consent, avoid implying guaranteed results, show realistic outcomes and avoid comparisons with other practitioners. Platforms may also restrict them in ads.

A Consultation-Led Funnel

  1. Educational content and search ads targeted at treatment research.
  2. A clear consultation offer — not discounted procedures.
  3. Fast follow-up and a structured consultation process.
  4. Aftercare and follow-up communication with consent.

Google Business Profile and Reviews

Keep your profile accurate and respond to reviews without confirming anyone is a patient or discussing treatment. See getting more reviews.

Privacy

Health information is special personal information under POPIA. Keep enquiry forms minimal and secure, and never send health details to ad platforms. See POPIA and marketing.

Common Mistakes

  • Promoting discounted injectables or "specials" on medicines.
  • Using superlatives such as "best clinic" or "top doctor".
  • Before-and-after posts without consent or with misleading lighting and angles.
  • Collecting health details through unsecured forms or social messages.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can aesthetic clinics advertise Botox in South Africa?

Prescription medicines are restricted in advertising to the public, and ad platforms restrict them too. Clinics typically advertise consultations and practitioners instead. Check with your professional body.

Can aesthetic clinics use before-and-after photos?

Only with care: written consent, realistic results, no implied guarantees and no superiority claims. Ad platforms may also restrict them.

What marketing works best for aesthetic clinics?

Educational content, practitioner credibility, a consultation-led offer, strong reviews and fast follow-up.
