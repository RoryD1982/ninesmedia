Local SEO in South Africa: How to Rank Your Google Business Profile in the Map Pack

Brandon — Nines Media 2026-07-10 8 min read

When someone searches "digital marketing agency Johannesburg" or "plumber near me" on Google, the map pack — those three business listings with stars and a map — appears above all other results. Ranking in those three spots is one of the most valuable pieces of digital real estate a local South African business can occupy. Here's how to get there.

What Is Local SEO?

Local SEO is the process of optimising your online presence so that you appear prominently in geographically-targeted searches — in Google Search results, in Google Maps, and in the map pack. It's different from regular SEO in that Google uses physical proximity, relevance, and prominence as the three core ranking factors, rather than purely content and backlinks.

Step 1: Claim and Complete Your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is the single most important local SEO asset. If you haven't claimed it, search for your business on Google Maps and claim it at business.google.com. Once claimed:

  • Choose the right primary category — be as specific as possible (e.g. "Digital Marketing Agency" not just "Marketing Agency")
  • Add all secondary categories that apply to your services
  • Write a keyword-rich description (750 characters) that mentions your services and locations served
  • Add your exact address and confirm it matches your website exactly
  • Add all service areas where you serve customers (for service-area businesses)
  • Add your opening hours, phone number, and website URL
  • Add photos — businesses with photos receive 42% more requests for directions

Step 2: Get More Google Reviews (The Right Way)

Reviews are one of the top local SEO ranking factors. More reviews, higher average rating, and recent reviews all contribute to ranking in the map pack. The most effective way to get reviews: ask satisfied customers directly, right after a successful job or interaction, and make it easy by sending them a direct link to your Google review page.

What you should never do: buy reviews, offer incentives for reviews (against Google's guidelines), or ask employees to leave reviews. Google detects and removes these, and repeated violations can result in your profile being suspended.

Respond to every review — positive and negative. Responding to negative reviews professionally signals trustworthiness to both Google and prospective customers.

Step 3: NAP Consistency Across the Web

NAP stands for Name, Address, Phone. Google cross-references your business details across the web. If your business name, address, or phone number appears differently on your website, Facebook, Gumtree, or local directories, it creates conflicting signals that can hurt your ranking. Audit your listings and ensure your NAP is identical everywhere — including punctuation.

Step 4: Create Location-Specific Website Pages

For businesses serving multiple cities or suburbs, create dedicated landing pages for each location. "Plumber in Sandton", "Plumber in Midrand", "Plumber in Fourways" — each as a separate, properly written page with unique content. These pages signal to Google that you genuinely serve those areas and give you a chance to rank in organic results alongside the map pack.

Step 5: Use Google Business Posts

Google Business Posts appear directly on your profile and can include offers, news, events, and product highlights. Active profiles with regular posts rank better than inactive ones. Post at least once a week — a short update, a seasonal offer, or a link to a new blog article. Keep posts fresh; they expire after 7 days unless you choose the "Event" format.

Tracking Your Local SEO Performance

Monitor your Google Business Profile Insights monthly: how many times your profile appeared in searches (impressions), how many people clicked for directions, website clicks, and phone calls. Google Search Console shows which search queries are driving impressions and clicks to your website. For map pack ranking, tools like BrightLocal (paid) or Google's own search (incognito, specific location) give you a view of where you rank.
