Local SEO in South Africa: How to Rank Your Google Business Profile in the Map Pack

Nines Media Team Updated 9 October 2026 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Google ranks local results on relevance, distance and prominence — you control relevance and prominence.
  • A complete Google Business Profile with the right primary category, services, photos and accurate hours is the biggest free win.
  • Reviews — volume, recency, and your replies — strongly influence both rankings and clicks.
  • Back your profile up with location pages on your website and consistent business details across directories.

When someone in Sandton searches "dentist near me" or someone in Durbanville searches "aircon repairs", Google usually shows a map with three businesses before the regular results. That map pack is often the most valuable spot on the page for local businesses — and it costs nothing to appear there. Here's how to win it.

How Google ranks local results

Google uses three main factors:

  1. Relevance — how well your profile matches the search.
  2. Distance — how close you are to the searcher or the location in the search.
  3. Prominence — how well known and trusted your business is, based on reviews, links, mentions and your website's strength.

You can't move your premises, but you can control relevance and prominence.

Step 1: Claim and verify your Google Business Profile

Search for your business on Google Maps or go to Google Business Profile Manager. Claim it, verify it, and make sure the account is owned by the business (not an employee's personal account or a former agency).

Step 2: Choose the right categories

Your primary category is the strongest relevance signal. Choose the most specific option ("Personal injury attorney" rather than "Law firm" if that's your main work). Add secondary categories for other genuine services.

Step 3: Complete every section

  • Business name exactly as it appears on your signage (no keyword stuffing — it violates guidelines).
  • Address or service area.
  • Phone number with a local number where possible.
  • Website link — ideally to the relevant location page.
  • Opening hours, including public holiday hours.
  • Services and products with descriptions and prices where possible.
  • Attributes (for example wheelchair accessible, women-led, online appointments).
  • Business description explaining what you do and where.

Step 4: Add real photos and video

Upload your exterior, interior, team, products and completed work. Add new photos regularly. Real images build trust far more than stock.

Step 5: Build a review engine

Reviews influence rankings and clicks. Focus on:

  • Volume — a steady flow, not a one-off burst.
  • Recency — recent reviews matter.
  • Detail — reviews mentioning services and suburbs help relevance.
  • Replies — respond to every review, positive and negative, professionally.

Make asking part of your process: send a direct review link by WhatsApp or SMS after each completed job, or use a QR code at your counter. Don't buy reviews or reward customers for positive ones.

Step 6: Strengthen your website's local signals

  • Create a page for each location or major service area with unique, useful content — not copy-paste pages with the suburb swapped.
  • Include your name, address and phone number, an embedded map, and local proof (projects, reviews, landmarks).
  • Add LocalBusiness structured data.
  • Link your Google Business Profile to the matching page.

Step 7: Keep your details consistent everywhere

Make sure your business name, address and phone number match across your website, Google, Facebook, Apple Maps, Bing Places, industry associations and South African directories. Inconsistent details confuse both Google and AI assistants.

Step 8: Use posts, Q&A and messaging

Share offers, updates and events with Google posts. Answer common questions on your profile and website, and respond to messages quickly.

Step 9: Track results

Google Business Profile performance shows searches, views, calls, direction requests and website clicks. Add UTM tags to your website link so GA4 can attribute visits correctly.

Common local SEO mistakes

  • Using a virtual office or PO box as your address (risks suspension).
  • Stuffing keywords into the business name.
  • Listing the wrong primary category.
  • Ignoring negative reviews.
  • Having two or more duplicate profiles.

Local SEO, ads and AI

Local SEO works best alongside Google Ads for local businesses, which can put you at the top of results instantly while your organic visibility grows. And because AI assistants increasingly answer "who's the best X near me?", consistent profiles and strong reviews also help you get recommended — see how to get recommended by ChatGPT and AI search.

Frequently asked questions

How do I rank higher on Google Maps in South Africa?

Choose the most accurate primary category, complete every section of your Google Business Profile, add real photos, collect steady reviews and reply to them, build a matching location page on your website, and keep your name, address and phone number consistent everywhere.

Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Creating and managing a Google Business Profile is free. You need to verify that you own or manage the business, usually by video, phone or email.

How do I get more Google reviews?

Ask every happy customer, at the right moment, with a direct review link or QR code. Make it part of your process — after a job is completed, an order is delivered or a consultation ends. Never buy reviews or offer incentives for positive ones.

Can I rank in areas where I don't have an office?

Your map pack visibility is strongest near your verified address or service area. For surrounding suburbs, create genuinely useful location pages on your website and consider Google Ads location targeting.

Do Google Business Profile posts help SEO?

Posts keep your profile fresh and can improve engagement and conversions. Their direct ranking effect is modest, but an active, complete profile tends to perform better.
