Digital Marketing for Dentists in South Africa: Fill Your Chair

By Brandon Howard2026-10-127 min read

Dentistry in South Africa is a competitive market. In most suburbs, a patient has three or four practices within driving distance — and they choose based on Google reviews, website quality, and convenience of booking. Here's how to make sure they choose you.

Where Dental Patients Come From Online

The primary digital channels for new patient acquisition in South African dental practices are:

  • Google Search Ads: "dentist near me", "dental implants [city]", "emergency dentist [suburb]"
  • Google Business Profile: The map pack is the first thing patients see when searching for a dentist locally
  • Instagram and Facebook: Before-and-after results (with patient consent), practice culture, and cosmetic dentistry promotions
  • SEO: Service pages targeting "teeth whitening [city]", "dental implants South Africa cost", "wisdom tooth removal [suburb]"

Google Ads for Dental Practices

Dental Google Ads work best when structured by treatment type. A campaign targeting "emergency dentist" should lead to a different landing page than one targeting "dental implants" — because the patient's intent, urgency, and decision timeline are completely different.

High-converting dental keywords in South Africa:

  • "dentist [suburb/city]" — general practice keywords
  • "dental implants [city]" — high-value cosmetic/restorative
  • "emergency dentist [suburb]" — urgent intent, high conversion
  • "teeth whitening [city]" — cosmetic, high volume
  • "orthodontist [city]" / "Invisalign [city]"

Google Business Profile for Dentists

Your GBP is often the first thing a prospective patient sees. Key optimisation steps:

  • List every service you offer as a GBP service item
  • Upload high-quality photos of your rooms, equipment, and staff
  • Respond to every review — positive and negative
  • Enable online booking directly from your GBP if your practice management software supports it
  • Post monthly updates about promotions or new services

Social Media for Dental Practices

Instagram and Facebook work well for cosmetic procedures — veneers, whitening, and orthodontics. Educational content works well: what a treatment involves, how long it takes and what it costs. Be careful with before-and-after images and patient testimonials — HPCSA rules prohibit misleading advertising and canvassing, so avoid anything implying guaranteed results or superiority, get written consent, and check with the HPCSA or SADA if unsure. Facebook advertising to targeted demographics (age, suburb, income) can fill cosmetic dentistry consultation slots efficiently.

Review strategy: Text patients a Google review link the same day as their appointment, when experience is fresh. A practice with 80+ reviews at 4.8 stars dominates local search results over a competitor with 12 reviews, regardless of which practice runs better ads.

Staying Within HPCSA Rules

Dentists are registered with the HPCSA, whose ethical rules allow advertising as long as it isn't unprofessional, untruthful, deceptive or misleading, and prohibit canvassing — such as claiming superior quality of service. In practice: describe treatments factually, avoid "best dentist" and guarantee claims, be careful with before-and-after images that imply guaranteed results, and check with the HPCSA or SADA if you're unsure.

Promote Treatments Separately

A general "dentist" campaign competes with everyone. Separate campaigns and landing pages for specific services let you target higher-value treatments and see which ones produce patients:

  • Emergency dental care and toothache (high urgency, call-focused).
  • Check-ups and cleaning for families.
  • Implants, crowns and bridges.
  • Clear aligners and orthodontics.
  • Teeth whitening and cosmetic treatments.

Each page should explain the treatment, what to expect, medical aid information and how to book.

Recalls and Reminders

Your existing patients are your most valuable marketing asset. Automated six-month recall reminders by SMS, email or WhatsApp (with consent), easy online rebooking, and follow-ups for incomplete treatment plans bring in revenue without any ad spend.

Respond to Reviews Carefully

Reply to Google reviews politely, but never confirm someone is a patient or discuss their treatment publicly — patient confidentiality applies online too.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can dentists advertise in South Africa?

Yes. HPCSA rules allow dentists to advertise if the advertising is truthful and not misleading. Canvassing, such as claiming superior quality of service, is prohibited.

What is the best way for a dental practice to get new patients online?

A complete Google Business Profile with reviews, Google Ads campaigns for specific treatments in your area, and a website with clear treatment and booking information.

How can dentists increase revenue from existing patients?

Automated recall reminders, easy online rebooking and follow-up on incomplete treatment plans bring in revenue without extra ad spend.
