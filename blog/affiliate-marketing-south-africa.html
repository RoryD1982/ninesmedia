Affiliate Marketing in South Africa: How It Works and How to Start

By Brandon Howard2026-10-127 min read

Affiliate marketing is one of the few digital marketing models where you only pay for results. For South African businesses, it's an underutilised growth channel. For individuals, it's a legitimate way to generate income online. This guide covers both sides.

How Affiliate Marketing Works

An affiliate programme allows third parties (affiliates) to promote your products or services in exchange for a commission on each sale or lead they generate. You pay only when the affiliate delivers a result — making it inherently low-risk compared to paid advertising where you pay per click regardless of outcome.

For Businesses: Setting Up an Affiliate Programme

Step 1: Choose a Platform

Popular affiliate management platforms used in SA include:

  • Impact.com: Enterprise-grade, used by major SA brands
  • Post Affiliate Pro: Affordable for SMEs
  • WooCommerce / Shopify plugins: For e-commerce stores
  • In-house tracking: Simple referral codes or UTM-tracked links for smaller programmes

Step 2: Set Commission Structure

South African affiliate commission rates vary by industry:

IndustryTypical Commission
E-commerce (products)5% – 15% of sale value
Financial services (leads)R200 – R800 per qualified lead
SaaS / software20% – 40% of subscription value
Travel and hospitality5% – 10% of booking value

Step 3: Recruit Affiliates

Target bloggers, content creators, comparison sites, and existing customers. The best affiliates are already talking to your target audience.

For Individuals: Starting as an Affiliate in South Africa

South Africans can earn from international and local affiliate programmes. High-performing categories include financial products, travel, e-commerce, and digital tools. Successful affiliates typically build one of: a niche website with SEO traffic, a YouTube channel, a social media following, or an email list.

Tax note: Affiliate income earned by South African residents is taxable. Keep records of all commissions received and declare income to SARS accordingly. Consult a tax practitioner if your earnings are significant.

Running a Successful Affiliate Programme

Launching a programme is easy; getting affiliates to actually promote you is the hard part. Programmes that work tend to have:

  • A competitive, clear commission. Affiliates compare programmes. State exactly what counts as a sale or qualified lead and when commission is paid.
  • Reliable tracking. Use a proper tracking platform with cookie windows of 30 days or more, so affiliates are credited when customers take time to decide.
  • Ready-made creative. Product images, banners, discount codes and short copy affiliates can use straight away.
  • Rules on brand bidding. Decide whether affiliates may bid on your brand name in Google Ads. Most businesses prohibit it, because it means paying commission on customers who were already searching for you.
  • Fraud checks. Watch for fake leads, cookie stuffing and self-referrals, and reserve the right to reverse commissions on cancelled or fraudulent orders.

Affiliate Marketing for Individuals: What Actually Works

Most affiliate income in South Africa comes from people who already have an audience: a niche blog that ranks on Google, a YouTube channel, an Instagram or TikTok following, or a newsletter. Pick a niche you know well, build useful content (reviews, comparisons, how-to guides), and recommend products you'd genuinely use. Expect it to take months to earn meaningfully. Be wary of schemes that promise quick income or charge you upfront to join.

Disclosure, Tax and POPIA

  • Disclose affiliate links. The Advertising Regulatory Board's code expects paid relationships to be clear to audiences, and platforms like Instagram and YouTube have their own disclosure tools.
  • Declare your income. Affiliate commissions are taxable income in South Africa.
  • Protect personal data. Businesses sharing customer or lead data with affiliates must handle it lawfully under POPIA.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is affiliate marketing legal in South Africa?

Yes. Affiliate marketing is legal, but affiliates should clearly disclose paid relationships, declare commission income for tax, and handle any personal data in line with POPIA.

How much commission do South African affiliate programmes pay?

It varies by industry: roughly 5–15% of sale value for physical products, 20–40% for software subscriptions, and fixed amounts per qualified lead in financial services.

How long does it take to make money from affiliate marketing?

Usually several months. Most earners first build an audience through a website, YouTube channel, social media account or newsletter before commissions become meaningful.
