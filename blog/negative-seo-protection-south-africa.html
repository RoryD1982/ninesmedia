Negative SEO: How to Protect Your South African Business Website

By Nines Media2026-10-126 min read

Negative SEO — the practice of deliberately trying to harm a competitor's search rankings — is a real risk for South African businesses in competitive digital markets. While relatively rare, knowing how to detect and defend against it can prevent serious damage to your organic search performance.

What Is Negative SEO?

Negative SEO involves hostile actions taken against a website to lower its Google rankings. Common tactics include:

  • Toxic link building: Pointing thousands of spammy backlinks at your domain from low-quality or blacklisted sites
  • Content scraping: Copying your content to dozens of other sites to create duplicate content issues
  • Fake reviews: Flooding your Google Business Profile with false negative reviews
  • Hacking: Injecting malicious content or redirects into your site
  • Crawl attacks: Overloading your server with crawl requests to degrade performance

How to Detect Negative SEO

Regular monitoring is your first defence. Key signals to watch:

  • Sudden spike in backlinks from low-quality domains (monitor in Google Search Console or Ahrefs)
  • Unexplained drop in organic rankings or traffic
  • Google Manual Action notification in Search Console
  • Duplicate content appearing on other domains (use Copyscape)
  • Sudden increase in negative Google reviews

How to Protect Your Site

Google Disavow Tool

If you discover a pattern of spammy, toxic backlinks pointing to your site, you can submit a disavow file to Google via Search Console. This tells Google to ignore those links when evaluating your site. Use this only when you have evidence of a harmful link pattern — indiscriminate disavowing can remove legitimate links.

Google Search Console Monitoring

Check your backlink profile monthly in Search Console. Set up email alerts for any Manual Actions. For higher-risk competitive sectors (legal, financial, real estate), check weekly.

Website Security

Keep your CMS (WordPress, etc.) and all plugins updated. Use a Web Application Firewall (WAF) like Cloudflare. Back up your site daily. A hacked and injected site is both a negative SEO vector and a direct risk to your customers.

Perspective: For most South African SMEs, genuine negative SEO attacks are rare. More common is the unintentional acquisition of bad links from previous SEO agencies using outdated tactics. An annual backlink audit is sufficient for most businesses; only high-profile competitive sectors need more frequent monitoring.
