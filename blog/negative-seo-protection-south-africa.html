Negative SEO: How to Protect Your South African Business Website

By Brandon Howard2026-10-126 min read

Negative SEO — the practice of deliberately trying to harm a competitor's search rankings — is a real risk for South African businesses in competitive digital markets. While relatively rare, knowing how to detect and defend against it can prevent serious damage to your organic search performance.

What Is Negative SEO?

Negative SEO involves hostile actions taken against a website to lower its Google rankings. Common tactics include:

  • Toxic link building: Pointing thousands of spammy backlinks at your domain from low-quality or blacklisted sites
  • Content scraping: Copying your content to dozens of other sites to create duplicate content issues
  • Fake reviews: Flooding your Google Business Profile with false negative reviews
  • Hacking: Injecting malicious content or redirects into your site
  • Crawl attacks: Overloading your server with crawl requests to degrade performance

How to Detect Negative SEO

Regular monitoring is your first defence. Key signals to watch:

  • Sudden spike in backlinks from low-quality domains (monitor in Google Search Console or Ahrefs)
  • Unexplained drop in organic rankings or traffic
  • Google Manual Action notification in Search Console
  • Duplicate content appearing on other domains (use Copyscape)
  • Sudden increase in negative Google reviews

How to Protect Your Site

Google Disavow Tool

If you discover a pattern of spammy, toxic backlinks pointing to your site, you can submit a disavow file to Google via Search Console. This tells Google to ignore those links when evaluating your site. Use this only when you have evidence of a harmful link pattern — indiscriminate disavowing can remove legitimate links.

Google Search Console Monitoring

Check your backlink profile monthly in Search Console. Set up email alerts for any Manual Actions. For higher-risk competitive sectors (legal, financial, real estate), check weekly.

Website Security

Keep your CMS (WordPress, etc.) and all plugins updated. Use a Web Application Firewall (WAF) like Cloudflare. Back up your site daily. A hacked and injected site is both a negative SEO vector and a direct risk to your customers.

Perspective: For most South African SMEs, genuine negative SEO attacks are rare. More common is the unintentional acquisition of bad links from previous SEO agencies using outdated tactics. An annual backlink audit is sufficient for most businesses; only high-profile competitive sectors need more frequent monitoring.

Is It Really Negative SEO?

Real negative SEO attacks are uncommon. Most ranking drops have other causes, so check these first:

  • A Google core or spam update around the time of the drop.
  • Technical changes: a redesign, new plugin, changed URLs, accidental noindex tags or robots.txt blocks.
  • Competitors publishing better content or earning stronger links.
  • Lost backlinks from sites that removed or changed pages.
  • Seasonal changes in search demand.

Search Console's Performance and Page Indexing reports usually show which pages and queries dropped, which narrows down the cause.

When to Disavow Links

Google says it generally ignores spammy links pointing at a site, so most sites never need the disavow tool. Consider disavowing only if you have a manual action for unnatural links in Search Console, or you know of a large volume of paid or manipulative links pointing at your site (for example from a previous SEO provider). Disavowing good links by mistake can hurt rankings, so be conservative.

Security Is Your Best Protection

The most damaging "negative SEO" is actually hacking: injected spam pages, hidden links and malicious redirects that get a site flagged or deindexed. For WordPress sites especially:

  • Keep core, themes and plugins updated, and remove unused plugins.
  • Use strong passwords and two-factor authentication for every admin.
  • Use a security plugin or firewall, and keep off-site backups.
  • Monitor Search Console for security issues and unexpected new pages.

Recovering From an Attack

Clean the site, close the vulnerability, remove spam pages (returning 404 or 410), request a review in Search Console if there's a security or manual action, and monitor indexing until it's back to normal. A website SEO audit is a good next step, or ask us for a free audit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is negative SEO common?

Real negative SEO attacks are rare. Most ranking drops are caused by algorithm updates, technical changes, stronger competitors or lost links.

Should I disavow spammy backlinks?

Usually not. Google generally ignores spammy links. Consider disavowing only if you have a manual action for unnatural links or know of large volumes of paid links pointing at your site.

How do I protect my website from negative SEO?

Keep your site secure and updated, use two-factor authentication, keep backups, and monitor Search Console for security issues, manual actions and unexpected new pages.
