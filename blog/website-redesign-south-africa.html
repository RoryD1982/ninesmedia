Website Redesign in South Africa: When to Do It and What It Costs

By Brandon Howard 2026-10-16 6 min read

Your website is often the first impression a potential customer has of your business. If that impression doesn't inspire confidence, they'll leave — and probably give their business to a competitor whose website looks like it was built this decade. A website redesign is one of the highest-ROI investments a South African business can make — but only if you do it for the right reasons, at the right time, with the right brief.

Signs You Need a Website Redesign

  • Your bounce rate is above 70% (people leave immediately after arriving)
  • The site loads slowly on mobile — Google's PageSpeed score below 50
  • It's not mobile-responsive (or looks broken on a smartphone)
  • You're embarrassed to share the URL
  • It hasn't been updated in 3+ years
  • You're not getting enquiries from it despite decent traffic
  • The brand has evolved but the website hasn't
  • Your competitors' websites make yours look outdated

What Should a Redesign Include?

A proper website redesign is not just a visual refresh. It should address:

  • Strategy: Who is the audience, what action should they take, and what content hierarchy achieves that?
  • SEO: Preserve existing rankings through proper 301 redirects and maintain or improve on-page optimisation
  • Speed: A new design should pass Core Web Vitals and load in under 3 seconds on mobile
  • Conversion: Clear CTAs, trust signals, and a contact journey that removes friction
  • Content: Refresh or rewrite copy — a new design with old copy is a waste of money

The Biggest Redesign Mistakes South African Businesses Make

  • Prioritising aesthetics over performance — a beautiful site that doesn't rank or convert is a vanity project
  • Not preserving existing SEO equity — a redesign without proper redirects can wipe out months of ranking progress overnight
  • Launching without testing on mobile — over 65% of SA web traffic is mobile
  • Not setting up Google Analytics and Search Console on the new site from day one
  • Paying for a redesign when what's really needed is a conversion fix — sometimes the issue is a weak headline and form, not the entire design

How Long Does a Website Redesign Take?

Realistic timelines for South African agencies:

  • Simple 5–8 page redesign: 4–6 weeks
  • Medium 10–20 page business site: 6–10 weeks
  • Complex site with custom features: 10–16 weeks

The biggest delays almost always come from the client side — waiting for copy, photos, and approvals. Have your content ready before development starts.

How to Redesign Without Losing Google Rankings

Redesigns are one of the most common causes of sudden traffic drops. Protect your rankings with these steps:

  1. Record your current performance — export top pages and queries from Search Console and GA4 before you start.
  2. Keep URLs where possible. If URLs must change, map every old URL to its new equivalent.
  3. Set up 301 redirects from every old URL to the new one — not all to the home page.
  4. Keep content that ranks. Improve it, but don't delete the pages and text bringing in traffic.
  5. Check technical basics before launch — no "noindex" left on from staging, correct canonical tags, working sitemap, analytics and conversion tracking installed.
  6. Monitor after launch — check Search Console for errors and compare traffic weekly for the first two months.

Redesign or Refresh?

Not every site needs a full rebuild. If the structure works but the site looks dated or converts poorly, a refresh — new design, faster templates, better landing pages, improved copy — is cheaper and lower risk. Rebuild when the platform is outdated or insecure, the site can't be updated easily, or the structure no longer fits your business.

Plan the Content, Not Just the Design

The most common cause of delays is waiting for content. Decide early who writes copy, supplies photos and approves pages. A website built around strong content and clear calls to action outperforms a beautiful site with vague text.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will a website redesign affect my Google rankings?

It can if URLs, content or technical settings change carelessly. Mapping old URLs to new ones with 301 redirects, keeping ranking content, and checking indexing settings before launch protects rankings.

How much does a website redesign cost in South Africa?

Most small business redesigns cost from around R10,000 to R60,000, depending on page count, custom design, content and functionality. Large or e-commerce sites cost more.

How long does a website redesign take?

A small business redesign typically takes four to eight weeks, with content preparation and approvals usually the biggest factors in the timeline.
