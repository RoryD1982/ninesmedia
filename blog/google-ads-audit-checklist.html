Google Ads Audit Checklist: 25 Things to Check in Your Account

By Brandon Howard 3 November 2026 4 min read
Key takeaways
  • Most wasted Google Ads spend comes from a handful of issues: broken tracking, broad targeting, missing negatives and poor location settings.
  • Check conversion tracking first — every other decision depends on it.
  • Run this audit every quarter, and before changing agencies or budgets.

Whether you manage Google Ads yourself or pay someone to, a regular audit catches waste before it compounds. Use this checklist to review your account. Work through it in order — the early sections affect everything after them.

1. Conversion Tracking

  1. Are form submissions tracked as conversions — and only real submissions, not button clicks?
  2. Are phone calls from ads and from your website tracked?
  3. Are WhatsApp clicks tracked?
  4. Is the primary conversion goal set to the action that matters most (a lead or sale), not page views?
  5. Do conversion numbers roughly match what your team actually receives?

If tracking is wrong, smart bidding optimises for the wrong thing. Fix this before anything else. See conversion tracking setup.

2. Account and Campaign Settings

  1. Location targeting set to "Presence: people in or regularly in your locations" — not "interest" — for local businesses.
  2. Locations limited to areas you actually serve.
  3. Search campaigns not opted into the Display Network by accident.
  4. Ad schedule matches when you can answer enquiries.
  5. Budgets aren't limiting your best campaigns while weak ones keep spending.

3. Keywords and Search Terms

  1. Review the search terms report: what share of spend went on irrelevant searches?
  2. Is there a shared negative keyword list (jobs, courses, free, DIY, salary, template)? See our starter list.
  3. Are keywords grouped tightly by theme, so ads match searches closely?
  4. Are broad match keywords only used where smart bidding has enough conversion data?
  5. Are you paying for your own brand name — and is that sensible?

4. Ads and Assets

  1. Each ad group has at least one responsive search ad with varied headlines.
  2. Headlines include the service, location and a clear reason to choose you.
  3. Sitelinks, callouts, call and location assets are active.
  4. Ad strength isn't "Poor" across the account.

5. Bidding and Budget

  1. Bid strategy matches conversion volume (smart bidding needs consistent conversions to work well).
  2. Target CPA or ROAS targets are realistic, not choking delivery.
  3. Cost per conversion by campaign is known and compared with what a customer is worth.

6. Landing Pages

  1. Each ad group sends people to the most relevant page — not the home page.
  2. Pages load quickly on mobile and show phone and WhatsApp options.
  3. The headline matches the search and ad that brought the visitor.

What to Do With Your Findings

Prioritise by money: fix tracking, then location and network settings, then search term waste, then ads and landing pages. Most accounts we audit find meaningful waste in the first three sections alone. Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I audit my Google Ads account?

A light review monthly and a full audit every quarter, plus before changing budgets or agencies.

What is the most common Google Ads mistake?

Broken or missing conversion tracking, followed closely by broad location targeting and a lack of negative keywords.

Can I audit Google Ads myself?

Yes. Use the search terms report, conversion settings, location settings and landing page checks in this list. An outside audit helps if you're too close to the account.
