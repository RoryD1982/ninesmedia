Should You Outsource Digital Marketing? A Guide for SA Business Owners

By Brandon Howard2026-10-127 min read

It's a question every growing South African business eventually faces: should we hire in-house, or outsource to a digital marketing agency? The right answer depends on your budget, growth stage, and how central digital is to your strategy. Here's a clear-eyed breakdown.

The Case for Outsourcing

  • Access to specialists: A good agency gives you access to a Google Ads specialist, an SEO strategist, a content writer, a designer, and a data analyst — without hiring five people
  • Speed: An experienced agency can have a Google Ads campaign running in days; recruiting, onboarding, and ramping a new employee takes months
  • No training cost: Digital platforms change constantly. Agencies stay current; keeping an in-house employee up to date requires ongoing training investment
  • Accountability: Agencies live and die by results. An employee who underperforms is harder to replace than an agency that misses targets

The Case for In-House

  • Brand knowledge: An in-house team lives and breathes your brand, culture, and customers
  • Speed on content: Day-to-day social media content and real-time community management is often better handled internally
  • Cost at scale: At high enough spend levels (R100k+/month in ad spend), the management fee percentage a agency charges can exceed the salary cost of an in-house specialist

The Hybrid Model (Most Common for SA Growth Businesses)

The most effective arrangement for many South African SMEs is a hybrid model: an agency manages strategic channels (Google Ads, SEO, paid social) while an internal marketing coordinator handles brand content, social media posting, and communications. This combines specialist expertise with brand authenticity.

Key question: What's the cost of getting it wrong? Hiring the wrong in-house person for 6 months and then having to start over is expensive. A trial month with an agency is a faster, lower-risk way to test capability and fit.

What to Outsource First

If you're not outsourcing everything, outsource the work that needs specialist skills or tools you can't justify in-house:

  • Paid media (Google Ads and Meta) — mistakes are expensive, platforms change constantly and agencies spread tool costs across clients.
  • Technical SEO and website development — occasional, specialist work that rarely needs a full-time person.
  • Video and design production — equipment and skills are expensive to keep in-house for occasional use.

Keep close to home: knowledge of your customers, approving messaging, and responding to leads quickly.

How to Manage an Outsourced Team Well

  • Agree targets in business terms — leads, cost per lead, sales — not activity.
  • Own your accounts. Ad accounts, analytics, domain and website should be in your name.
  • Share sales feedback. Tell the agency which leads became customers so they can optimise for quality.
  • Have a regular rhythm — a monthly report and call, with a quarterly strategy review.
  • Appoint one internal owner who makes decisions and approves work quickly.

Signs It's Time to Bring Some Work In-House

When you're producing content daily, running many campaigns across several markets, or your agency fees approach the cost of a full-time specialist, a hybrid model often makes sense: hire an in-house marketing manager to own strategy and content, and keep an agency for paid media, SEO and specialist work. Frequently Asked Questions

Is it cheaper to outsource digital marketing in South Africa?

For most SMEs, yes. An agency retainer typically costs less than a full-time digital marketer's salary plus tools and training, and gives you access to several specialists.

What digital marketing should I keep in-house?

Keep customer knowledge, approvals and fast lead response in-house. Specialist work like paid media, technical SEO and video production is usually more cost-effective to outsource.

How do I make sure an outsourced agency delivers?

Agree targets in business terms, keep ownership of your accounts, share feedback on lead quality, and review results monthly with a quarterly strategy check-in.
