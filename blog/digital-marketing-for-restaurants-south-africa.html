Digital Marketing for Restaurants in South Africa: What Actually Works

By Brandon Howard 2026-10-17 6 min read

Running a restaurant in South Africa has always been tough. Load-shedding, rising food costs, and fierce competition make it tougher. But the restaurants that are thriving have something in common: they treat digital marketing as seriously as they treat their menu.

Here's what actually works for South African restaurants — not generic international advice, but strategies calibrated to how South Africans discover and choose where to eat.

Priority 1: Google Business Profile (Non-Negotiable)

When someone searches "restaurants in Parkhurst" or "best pizza Stellenbosch", the first results they see are Google Business Profiles — not websites. Your GBP needs to be complete and active:

  • Accurate opening hours (update for public holidays)
  • Current menu link or uploaded menu PDF
  • At least 20 high-quality food and ambience photos
  • Weekly Google Posts with specials or events
  • Respond to every review — especially the negative ones

A restaurant with 200+ reviews and a 4.4+ star rating will get chosen over a competitor with 30 reviews every time, regardless of the actual quality.

Priority 2: Instagram as Your Digital Menu

Food photos drive bookings. South Africans discover restaurants on Instagram more than any other social platform. Practical approach:

  • Post 4–5 times per week — food, drinks, ambience, team
  • Use Reels of the kitchen, cocktail preparation, and table service — these get dramatically more reach than static posts
  • Tag your suburb and city in every post
  • Use location-specific hashtags (#SandtonEats, #CapeTownFood, #JoziRestaurants)
  • Respond to every comment and DM within 24 hours

Priority 3: Food Delivery Platforms

Uber Eats and Mr D (formerly Mr Delivery) dominate South African food delivery. If you offer delivery, being on these platforms is table stakes. To perform well:

  • Invest in professional food photography — thumbnail photos drive click-through
  • Maintain a 4.5+ rating through order accuracy and packaging quality
  • Run platform promotions during off-peak hours to maintain volume
  • Use the data from these platforms to understand your most popular dishes and peak ordering times

Priority 4: Google Ads for Restaurants

Most SA restaurants don't run Google Ads — which means those that do have a significant advantage. Target high-intent searches like "restaurant [suburb] for dinner" and "best [cuisine type] [city]". A budget of R2,000–R5,000/month is typically enough for a single-location restaurant to drive meaningful additional covers.

Priority 5: Email and WhatsApp for Regulars

Your most valuable customers are the ones who've already been. Build a database through:

  • Reservation system email capture
  • WiFi sign-in (require email)
  • In-store competition or loyalty programme

Then use WhatsApp broadcasts or email to share weekly specials, events, and exclusive offers. Regular customers who feel like insiders spend more and refer more.

What Doesn't Work

Skip Facebook posting without a paid boost (organic reach is near-zero), TripAdvisor as a primary focus (declining relevance in SA), and expensive print/flyer campaigns (ROI is almost impossible to measure and rarely positive).
