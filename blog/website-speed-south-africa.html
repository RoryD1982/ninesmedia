Why Your Website Is Slow (and How to Fix It): A South African Guide

By Brandon Howard 3 November 2026 4 min read
Key takeaways
  • Most slow sites are caused by large images, too many plugins and scripts, or weak hosting.
  • Test on a real phone using mobile data, not office Wi-Fi.
  • Faster pages convert more visitors and make your ad spend go further.

Many South Africans browse on mid-range phones and prepaid data. A website that feels fine on office fibre can be painfully slow for them. Slow pages lose visitors before they see your offer — and that costs you leads and wastes ad spend.

How to Test Your Speed

  • PageSpeed Insights — Google's free tool, with mobile and desktop scores and real-user Core Web Vitals data where available.
  • Search Console's Core Web Vitals report — groups pages with similar problems.
  • A real phone on mobile data — the most honest test of all.

The Most Common Causes

1. Large images

Photos uploaded straight from a camera or phone can be several megabytes each. Resize images to the size they're displayed, compress them, and use modern formats like WebP.

2. Too many plugins and scripts

Each chat widget, tracker, slider and social feed adds code. Remove what you don't use, and load non-essential scripts after the main content.

3. Weak hosting

Overloaded shared hosting slows everything down. See choosing web hosting.

4. Heavy themes and page builders

Some multipurpose themes load large amounts of code on every page. Lighter themes or custom builds are faster.

5. No caching

Caching serves pre-built pages instead of building each one for every visitor. Most good hosts and WordPress caching plugins handle this.

6. Auto-playing video and large sliders

Especially costly on mobile data. Use a poster image and load video only when clicked.

Core Web Vitals in Plain English

  • Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) — how quickly the main content appears.
  • Interaction to Next Paint (INP) — how quickly the page responds to taps and clicks.
  • Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) — whether things jump around as the page loads.

Why Speed Matters for Marketing

Slow landing pages lose visitors you've paid for and can affect ad quality assessments. Faster pages generally convert better — making speed one of the most cost-effective improvements you can make. See landing page optimisation and Quality Score.

Quick Wins Checklist

  1. Compress and resize the largest images on your key pages.
  2. Remove unused plugins and scripts.
  3. Enable caching.
  4. Replace auto-playing video with a click-to-play image.
  5. Re-test on a phone using mobile data.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my website so slow?

The most common causes are large images, too many plugins and scripts, weak hosting, heavy themes and no caching.

How fast should my website load?

Aim for the main content to appear within about 2.5 seconds on mobile, which is Google's guideline for a good Largest Contentful Paint.

Does website speed affect Google rankings?

Page experience, including Core Web Vitals, is one factor among many. Speed has a bigger direct effect on conversions and ad performance.
